## Findings

Eleven of the thirteen final claims are supported by a passage in the saved final context, and the stored verdict is still not `SUPPORTED`. Those are cases 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, and 12. Cases 8, 9, and 10 are verbatim or a verbatim prefix. Cases 2 and 3 have a final claim that is a verbatim sentence after the second generation. Cases 11 and 12 show why a missing normalized substring is not the same as an unsupported claim: the date and the SAP Center location are in the passages under different wording.

Two cases must stay separate from that pattern.

- Case 6 is a wrong second short answer. The final claim says Elway's last game was Super Bowl XXXII. The saved passage says it was Super Bowl XXXIII. The stored `CONTRADICTED` verdict agrees with that final claim. The first generation's short answer and claim match the passage, but the premises used for round 0 are not stored.
- Case 4 also changes the short answer, from "Modern English" to "Old English". "Old English" does not match the gold answer. The two final claims are still quotations of the passage, and both verifier labels disagree with that reading. A wrong short answer and an unsupported claim are different failures here.
- Case 7's second short answer "2" fails a string match to "two" while the verified claim still says "two" and quotes the passage.
- Case 13's short answer matches a gold string, but the full claim states that graph isomorphism is NP-intermediate. The passage says "believed to be" and also says the classification is unsolved. The stored `CONTRADICTED` verdict is stronger than those passages.

The entailment scores do not cluster just below 0.50. Only case 5 is close, at 0.484, and its stored contradiction is 0.984. The other supported-looking rejections have entailment from about 0.000 to 0.223, with contradiction near 1.0 on the contradicted rows. Lowering the 0.50 support threshold would not accept those verbatim claims. This reading does not justify a threshold change.

What the records still cannot show is which premise produced the maximum contradiction. Joint verification takes the maximum entailment and the maximum contradiction independently, and the JSONL does not store per-premise probabilities or the premise responsible for the contradiction. Several of these claims sit beside unrelated chunks in the same final context. A high contradiction score can come from a different chunk than the sentence that states the claim. Round-0 disagreement also cannot be checked, because the first retrieval text was not saved.
